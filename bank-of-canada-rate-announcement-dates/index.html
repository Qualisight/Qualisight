What the Bank of Canada rate means for you

The Bank of Canada sets the rate big banks pay to borrow from each other overnight. Banks then set their own prime rate, and they almost always move it by the same amount. Prime is what variable-rate mortgages, lines of credit and home equity lines of credit are priced from.

Fixed-rate mortgages do not change until you renew. Most credit cards have a set rate that is not tied to prime. Savings account and GIC rates often follow the Bank of Canada too, but more slowly.

Paying down a variable-rate debt is the one move that helps whichever way rates go. See how fast with the debt payoff calculator, or plan the payment in the mortgage affordability calculator.

Common questions

When is the next Bank of Canada rate announcement?

Wednesday, December 9, 2026, at 9:45 a.m. Eastern time. After that, the first decision of 2027 is Wednesday, January 27, with the Monetary Policy Report.

What is the Bank of Canada interest rate right now?

2.50%. The Bank raised its policy rate by 0.25% on October 28, 2026, its first increase in more than three years. Before that it had held at 2.25% since October 2025.

What is the prime rate in Canada right now?

4.70% at the big banks. Banks set their own prime rate, but they usually move it by the same amount as the Bank of Canada, often the next day. RBC raised its prime from 4.45% to 4.70% on October 29, 2026.

What time does the Bank of Canada announce rates?

9:45 a.m. Eastern time, on eight set Wednesdays each year. Four of them come with the Monetary Policy Report: January, April, July and October.

Does a Bank of Canada rate change affect my credit card?

Usually not directly. Most Canadian credit cards have a set rate that is not tied to prime. It mostly affects variable-rate mortgages, lines of credit and home equity lines of credit.

Does it change my fixed-rate mortgage?

Not until you renew. A fixed rate stays the same for your term. At renewal, the rates on offer will reflect where rates are by then.
