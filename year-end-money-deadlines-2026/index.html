Year-end money deadlines for 2026

Four money moves in Canada have a December 31 deadline, and a fifth, selling investments at a loss, ends a day earlier. Here is each date, what it means in plain words, and the official source.

Last checked November 25, 2026. Information, not financial advice.

Wednesday, December 30, 2026

Last day to sell investments at a loss for 2026

Trades in Canada settle one business day later, so a sale on December 30 settles on December 31 and counts for 2026. A sale on December 31 settles in January and counts for 2027. This only matters in a regular (non-registered) account: losses inside a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA cannot be claimed.

Who it matters for: Anyone with investments in a regular account

Source: Raymond James, 2026 tax-loss guide
Thursday, December 31, 2026

Take money out of your TFSA now, not in January, if you need it soon

A TFSA withdrawal only becomes new room on January 1 of the next year. Take it out by December 31 and you get the room back on January 1, 2027. Wait until January and you wait until 2028. Never put a withdrawal back in the same year unless you know you have room.

Who it matters for: Anyone planning a TFSA withdrawal in the next few months

Source: Canada Revenue Agency: withdrawing from a TFSA
Thursday, December 31, 2026

Open an FHSA to start your 2026 room

FHSA room only starts the year you open your first account. Open one by December 31 and you get $8,000 of 2026 room, which can carry into 2027 even if you put nothing in yet. See the FHSA limit for 2027.

Who it matters for: First-time home buyers who have not opened one

Source: Canada Revenue Agency: First Home Savings Account
Thursday, December 31, 2026

Put money in an RESP to get the 2026 grant

The government adds 20% on the first $2,500 put into a child's RESP each year, up to $500. Contributions made by December 31 count for 2026. Missed basic grant room carries forward, but the extra grant for lower and middle income families does not.

Who it matters for: Parents and grandparents saving for school

Source: Government of Canada: RESP grant amounts
Thursday, December 31, 2026

Make donations you want to claim on your 2026 taxes

To claim a donation on your 2026 return, it must be made by December 31 and you need an official receipt from a registered charity. Donations you do not claim this year can be claimed in any of the next five years.

Who it matters for: Anyone giving to charity

Source: Canada Revenue Agency: charitable donation tax credit
Friday, January 1, 2027

New TFSA room arrives

The 2027 TFSA limit is added to everyone 18 or older, plus whatever you took out during 2026. The CRA has not published the 2027 figure yet: see the TFSA limit 2027 page, which updates the day it does, and work out your total with the TFSA room calculator.

Who it matters for: Every TFSA holder

Source: Canada Revenue Agency: TFSA contribution room
Monday, March 1, 2027

RRSP deadline for your 2026 taxes

Contributions made up to March 1, 2027 can be deducted on your 2026 return. See your limit on the RRSP limit page.

Who it matters for: Anyone using an RRSP to lower their 2026 taxes

Source: Canada Revenue Agency: RRSP limits

Common questions

What is the tax-loss selling deadline for 2026 in Canada?

Wednesday, December 30, 2026. Canadian trades settle one business day later, so a December 30 sale settles on December 31 and counts for 2026. It only applies to regular (non-registered) accounts.

Should I withdraw from my TFSA before December 31?

If you were already planning to take money out soon, doing it by December 31 gets the room back on January 1, 2027 instead of January 1, 2028. There is no reason to withdraw just because of the date.

Is there a TFSA contribution deadline?

No. Unused TFSA room carries forward with no deadline. The only year-end timing that matters is for withdrawals.

When is the RRSP deadline for 2026?

March 1, 2027. Contributions up to that date can be deducted on your 2026 return.

Is this financial advice?

No. This page lists dates and rules from official and named sources so you can plan. Whether a move makes sense depends on your situation.
