How lenders work out what you can afford

A Canadian lender checks two ratios, using a payment worked out at the stress test rate, not your real rate:

RatioWhat it countsLimit (insured)
GDSMortgage payment, property tax, heat, half of condo fees39% of gross income
TDSAll of the above plus every other debt payment44% of gross income

The most you can borrow is where the first of these limits is reached, or where your down payment stops meeting the minimum, whichever comes first. The calculator tells you which one is holding you back.

The stress test rate

You must qualify at the higher of 5.25% or your rate plus 2%. At a 4.5% rate you are tested at 6.5%. At 3% you are tested at 5.25%. Your real payment is at your real rate; the test only decides how much you can borrow. Switching lenders at renewal without borrowing more no longer needs the test.

A worked example

A first-time buyer with $95,000 household income, $300 a month of other debt payments, and $60,000 down, at a 4.5% rate over 30 years. The stress test rate is 6.5% (4.5% plus 2). A lender could approve about $459,000. The mortgage would be $412,167 including insurance, and the real payment about $2,078 a month. Income is the limit here: housing costs reach 39.0% of income, while all debts are at 42.7%. A more comfortable target with room to spare is about $384,000. Those are the numbers the calculator starts with. Swap in your own.

Down payment and insurance rules

The minimum down payment is 5% of the first $500,000 and 10% of the rest, up to $1.5 million. At $1.5 million or more it is 20% and mortgage insurance is not available. With less than 20% down, a one-time insurance premium of 2.8% to 4% is added to the mortgage, plus 0.2% for a 30-year amortization. Common questions

What is the mortgage stress test?

Lenders check that you could still make your payments at a higher rate. For uninsured mortgages at federally regulated lenders, OSFI sets that rate as the higher of 5.25% or your rate plus 2%. Insured mortgages use the same rule.

What are GDS and TDS?

GDS is your housing costs (mortgage at the stress test rate, property tax, heat and half of condo fees) as a share of your gross monthly income. TDS adds every other debt payment. For insured mortgages the limits are 39% and 44%.

How much house can I afford on $100,000 a year?

It depends on your debts, down payment and rate. Type 100000 into the calculator with your own numbers. Debts matter a lot: every $100 a month of other payments lowers the limit by thousands of dollars.

Can I get a 30-year mortgage?

With 20% or more down, yes. With less than 20% down, 30 years is allowed for first-time buyers and for new builds since December 15, 2024. Otherwise insured mortgages are capped at 25 years.

Does the bank have to approve the maximum?

No. This is the ceiling under the ratio rules. Lenders also look at your credit, job history and the property, and many people choose to borrow less.

What this calculator assumes

It uses the stress test rate, CMHC's 39% GDS and 44% TDS limits, CMHC's standard premiums, and payments with interest compounded twice a year as Canadian mortgages are. Property tax defaults to 1% of the price and heat to $120 a month; change them for your city. Lenders can be stricter, and uninsured lenders can use different limits.
