How TFSA room works

Your TFSA room is how much you can put in without paying a penalty. The CRA works it out like this:

This year's limit + room you did not use in past years + what you took out last year, minus what you put in this year.

You start getting room in the year you turn 18, as long as you live in Canada. You get the full year's limit even if your birthday is in December. Room you do not use never expires. It just carries forward.

If you were 18 or older and living in Canada every year since 2009, that adds up to $109,000 in 2026. The limit rises with inflation in steps of $500, so it does not change every year.

A worked example

Sam was born in 1998, so Sam turned 18 in 2016 and has room from 2016 on. That is $68,000 by 2026. Sam has put in $20,000 in total and took out $3,000 back in 2024. The $3,000 came back on January 1, 2025. So Sam's room is $68,000 minus $20,000 plus $3,000, which is $51,000. Those are the numbers the calculator starts with. Swap in your own.

Common questions

What is the TFSA limit for 2026?

$7,000, the same as 2024 and 2025. It is added to your room on January 1.

How much room do I have if I have never contributed?

Add up the yearly limits from the year you turned 18 (or 2009, if you turned 18 before then). Someone eligible every year since 2009 has $109,000 in 2026.

When do withdrawals come back as room?

On January 1 of the next year. If you take money out and put it back in the same year, it can push you over your room. This is the most common way people over-contribute.

What happens if I put in too much?

The CRA charges a tax of 1% per month on the highest amount you are over, for each month it stays there. Take the extra out as soon as you notice, and check your room in CRA My Account.

Does moving money between my own TFSAs use up room?

Not if the bank does a direct transfer for you. If you take the money out yourself and put it in the other TFSA, the deposit counts as a new contribution.

Why is my number different from CRA My Account?

My Account shows your room as of January 1 and does not include anything you did this year. Banks can also take a few weeks to report. If the two disagree, trust your own records and call the CRA.

What this calculator assumes

It uses the yearly limits the CRA has published up to 2026. It counts the full limit for the year you turn 18 and for any year you lived in Canada for part of the year. It assumes you have a social insurance number and that all deposits were normal contributions. It does not know about money you put back the same year or any special cases, so use it as a check, not the final word. This page is information, not tax or investment advice.
