How the two methods work

Both methods start the same way: you pay at least the monthly payment on every debt. The difference is where your extra money goes.

Snowball: smallest balance first

All extra money goes to the debt with the smallest balance. When it is gone, its payment rolls onto the next smallest, so your payments grow like a snowball. You clear whole debts sooner, which feels good and helps many people keep going.

Avalanche: highest interest first

All extra money goes to the debt with the highest interest rate. This never costs more interest than snowball, and it often costs less. The first win can take longer if your highest-rate debt is also a big one.

Common questions

What is the debt snowball method?

You pay the minimum on every debt, then put all your extra money on the smallest balance. When it is gone, its payment rolls onto the next smallest. You get quick wins, which helps many people keep going.

What is the debt avalanche method?

You pay the minimum on every debt, then put all your extra money on the debt with the highest interest rate. It usually costs the least interest overall.

Which is better, snowball or avalanche?

Avalanche never costs more interest than snowball, and often costs less. Snowball clears individual debts sooner, which can make it easier to stick with. The best method is the one you will keep doing.

Why does the calculator say my debt will never be paid off?

If a payment is smaller than the interest added each month, the balance grows. Raise the payment above the monthly interest and the plan can finish.

What this calculator assumes

Interest is added once a month at one twelfth of the yearly rate, rates stay the same, you make every payment, and you add no new debt. Real lenders can calculate interest daily and change rates, so treat the dates as close estimates. This page is information, not financial advice.
