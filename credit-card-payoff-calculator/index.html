Why the minimum payment takes so long

A minimum payment is a small slice of what you owe. As the balance drops, the minimum drops with it, so most of each payment keeps going to interest. That is why a card paid at the minimum can take many years to clear even if you never use it again. A fixed payment does not shrink, so every month more of it goes to the balance itself.

By law, federally regulated card issuers must show on your statement how long the balance would take to pay off if you paid only the minimum. Source: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada.

A worked example

A $5,000 balance at 20.99%, paid at $200 a month, is gone in about 34 months and costs about $1,632 in interest. Pay $250 a month and it takes about 25 months and about $1,207 in interest. Paid at a 3% minimum, the same card takes over 22 years and costs about $6,697 in interest. Those are the numbers the calculator starts with.

This calculator matches the Government of Canada's own example: $1,000 at 18% with a 3% or $10 minimum takes 10 years and $798.89 in interest, or 11 months and $91.62 at $100 a month. Try it with the FCAC credit card payment calculator.

Ways to pay it off faster

  • Set a fixed monthly payment instead of paying the minimum, and automate it.
  • Stop adding new charges to the card while you pay it down.
  • Ask your card issuer for a lower rate, or compare a lower-rate card. A balance transfer can help if the fees and the promo period work for you.
  • With more than one debt, pay every minimum and put the extra on one debt at a time. The debt payoff calculator compares the two common orders.
  • If payments feel unmanageable, a non-profit credit counsellor can go over your options for free.

Common questions

How long does it take to pay off $5,000 on a credit card?

At 20.99% interest, paying $200 a month takes about 34 months and costs about $1,632 in interest. Paying only a minimum of 3% of the balance would take over 22 years and cost about $6,697 in interest.

How is the minimum payment worked out in Canada?

Most card issuers outside Quebec use a small percentage of your balance, often around 3%, or a fixed amount such as $10, whichever is more. Your credit agreement says which method your card uses.

Does my statement show how long the minimum takes?

Yes. Federally regulated card issuers must show on your statement how long it would take to pay off the balance if you pay only the minimum.

Does paying a little more really help?

Yes. In the Government of Canada's example, $1,000 at 18% takes 10 years and $798.89 in interest at a 3% minimum, but 11 months and $91.62 at $100 a month.

I have more than one card or loan. Which do I pay first?

Pay every minimum, then put extra money on one debt at a time. The debt payoff calculator compares paying the smallest balance first with paying the highest rate first.
