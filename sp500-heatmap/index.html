How to read this heatmap

Each box is one company. A bigger box means a bigger share of the S&P 500. Green means the price went up over the period you picked, red means it went down, and grey means it barely moved. The brighter the colour, the bigger the move, up to 3% or more.

Boxes are grouped by sector, so you can see at a glance whether a whole part of the market moved together. Switch to 1 week or 1 month to see the bigger picture instead of one day's noise.

What is the S&P 500?

The S&P 500 is an index of about 500 large companies listed in the United States, run by S&P Dow Jones Indices. Some companies have two share classes, so the index holds slightly more than 500 stocks.

Many Canadians own it through funds listed in Toronto, which hold the same companies. Those funds' prices are in Canadian dollars, so their daily moves also include the US dollar exchange rate. This map shows the companies themselves, in US dollars.

Questions

What is an S&P 500 heatmap?

A picture of the US stock market where each company is a box. Bigger boxes are bigger parts of the index. Green boxes went up, red boxes went down, and the stronger the colour, the bigger the move.

Why is technology so big on the map?

Box size follows each company's weight in the S&P 500. A handful of very large technology companies make up a big share of the index, so the Information Technology block takes up much of the map.

How often does this heatmap update?

About every 20 minutes while the market is open on weekdays, and once more after the close. Prices can be delayed, so check your brokerage before you trade.

Why does my S&P 500 fund in Canada show a different move?

A fund priced in Canadian dollars also moves with the US dollar exchange rate, and some funds hedge that away. This map shows the companies in US dollars.

Is this investment advice?

No. It shows what prices did. It does not say what to buy or sell.
