How much is enough?

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada suggests saving 3 to 6 months of your regular expenses, or 3 to 6 months of income. Read its guide. Pick the low end if your income is steady, and lean toward 6 months if your pay changes from month to month or one income supports the whole household.

Where to keep it

Keep it somewhere you can reach quickly and will not touch for everyday spending, like a separate savings account with low or no fees. The FCAC explains why. Paying for a surprise from savings is usually better than paying with credit. Source.

Common questions

How much should I have in an emergency fund in Canada?

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada suggests saving the equivalent of 3 to 6 months of your regular expenses. You can also aim for 3 to 6 months of income. If you spend $3,000 a month, 3 months is $9,000 and 6 months is $18,000.

How long will it take to save my emergency fund?

Subtract what you have saved from your target, then divide by what you can put aside each month. At $250 a month, a $9,000 target from zero takes 36 months. The calculator above shows the exact month for your numbers.

Where should I keep my emergency fund?

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada suggests a savings account or an investment that can be easily cashed, kept separate so it is not touched unless there is an emergency. Look for low or no fees.

What counts as an emergency?

Things like a car repair, an urgent vet visit, losing your job, health problems that stop you from working, or a home repair such as a broken hot water tank. A sale or a trip is a savings goal, not an emergency.

What if I can only save a little?

Start small and keep going. At $5 a week you have $260 after a year, and at $20 a week you have $1,040. Cutting one regular expense and saving that amount helps the fund grow faster.

Why have an emergency fund if I have a credit card?

Paying for a surprise with savings is usually better than paying with credit, according to the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada. It keeps one bad month from turning into debt that costs interest.
