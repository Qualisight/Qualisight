How to read this heatmap

Each box is one company. A bigger box means a bigger share of the S&P/TSX 60. Green means the price went up over the period you picked, red means it went down, and grey means it barely moved. The brighter the colour, the bigger the move, up to 3% or more.

Boxes are grouped by sector, so you can see at a glance whether a whole part of the market moved together. Switch to 1 week or 1 month to see the bigger picture instead of one day's noise.

What is the S&P/TSX 60?

The S&P/TSX 60 is an index of 60 large companies listed on the Toronto Stock Exchange. It is run by S&P Dow Jones Indices and is the Canadian part of the S&P Global 1200. Banks, energy companies and miners make up a large share of it, which is why the Financials, Energy and Materials blocks on this map are so big.

You cannot buy the index itself, but index funds such as XIU hold all 60 stocks in the same proportions.

Questions

What is a TSX heatmap?

A picture of the Canadian stock market where each company is a box. Bigger boxes are bigger parts of the index. Green boxes went up, red boxes went down, and the stronger the colour, the bigger the move.

Why are the banks so big on the map?

Box size follows each company's weight in the S&P/TSX 60. Canada's big banks are some of the largest companies on the Toronto Stock Exchange, so they take up a large share of the index and of the map.

How often does this heatmap update?

About every 20 minutes while the market is open on weekdays, and once more after the close. Prices can be delayed, so check your brokerage before you trade.

Where do the weights and sectors come from?

From the daily holdings file of the iShares S&P/TSX 60 Index ETF (XIU), which holds every stock in the index. Prices come from Yahoo Finance public data.

Is this investment advice?

No. It shows what prices did. It does not say what to buy or sell.
